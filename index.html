Research

Tracing World Knowledge into Embodied Action in VLA Models

2026 – Present

Capstone research · Team lead

Leading a four-person team studying why VLA models fail to translate pretrained world knowledge into correct physical actions. Designed matched-control experiments on OpenVLA to distinguish knowledge-retrieval failures from visual-grounding failures.

Technical details
  • Hold the scene, layout, and initial state fixed while varying only the instruction.
  • Built an evaluation set in place of unreleased Act2Answer assets, audited it for answer leakage, and added an information-free control with decision criteria fixed before experiments.
  • Generated image-based VQA training pairs across 10 categories; comparing supervised fine-tuning, distillation with the model’s own VLM as teacher, and self-distillation fine-tuning.

AsyncMixVLA

2026

UC Irvine · Efficient and robust VLA inference

A system that runs a lightweight robot policy by default and switches to a stronger policy when a learned verifier predicts failure. Asynchronous inference and aligned handoff context reduce switching overhead.

Result: Policy-switch latency reduced from ~271 ms to 2–26 ms in evaluated configurations, with comparable task performance.

Paper submitted to DATE 2027.

Technical details

Identified complementary failure modes of VLA-Adapter and OpenVLA-OFT under environmental perturbations on LIBERO manipulation benchmarks. Aligned visual and proprioceptive observations at policy handoff to support adaptive switching.

Continual Learning for Robot Policies (FM-SDFT)

2026

Capstone research · February – July

Adapted self-distillation fine-tuning to continuous-action diffusion policies. Approximated the KL-divergence term using the flow-matching velocity gap between a frozen previous-task teacher and the current student.

Result: Average success rate on LIBERO improved from 72.0% to 80.5% over experience replay; forgetting (NBT) decreased from 0.223 to 0.171.

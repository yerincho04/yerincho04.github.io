Tracing World Knowledge into Embodied Action in VLA Models

2026 – Present

Capstone research · Team lead · OpenVLA / Act2Answer diagnostics

GitHubExperiment designVQA datasetExperiment report

Leading a four-person team investigating where pretrained world knowledge breaks down on the path to physical action. Built diagnostics on top of Act2Answer and OpenVLA to distinguish instruction understanding, visual grounding, action intent, and motor execution.

Diagnostic pipeline and dataset construction

Matched experiments hold the question, images, layout, random seed, robot initialization, and correct physical target fixed while changing only the instruction: a knowledge question, an explicit object name, or an explicit target location.

The figure above comes from the later photo-experiment report, which combines 360 local episodes with team results. Its probe analyses did not establish that recoverable world knowledge was being lost specifically at grounding; the work remains diagnostic.

  • Earlier matched pilot: the documented pilot contains 240 unique rollouts and 80 complete matched triplets across original and swapped layouts, with no initialization mismatches.
  • Layerwise analysis: capture pre-action hidden states at four backbone depths and train linear probes for target-side decoding. Group original and swapped versions of each question into the same split to prevent leakage.
  • Failure analysis: combine held-out probe predictions, end-effector motion, and final object placement to examine the relationship between decoded target information, movement toward a target, and successful placement.
  • Visual controls: the photo VQA v2 dataset documents 100 pairs each for state, material attributes, and emotion. Pairs compare the same object category while varying the tested property, with manually reviewed contact sheets and recorded image sources.
  • Training direction: compare supervised fine-tuning, distillation using the model’s own VLM as teacher, and self-distillation fine-tuning. The pilot’s target-side probes are diagnostic measurements, not evidence of general semantic understanding.

AsyncMixVLA

2026

UC Irvine · Advisor: Prof. Nikil Dutt · Paper submitted to DATE 2027

GitHubEvaluation report

Adaptive robot-policy orchestration that runs VLA-Adapter by default and invokes OpenVLA-OFT for recovery. A learned trigger uses front and wrist RGB, robot proprioception, Adapter actions, and their history; asynchronous execution and context alignment prepare the stronger policy for handoff.

Released benchmark results and evaluation scope

The released system includes a continuous two-stage trigger, visual and state alignment, a learned residual correction, and reproducible evaluation manifests. The learned trigger uses observations available at deployment; an oracle trigger is evaluated separately as an upper bound.

Continual Learning for Robot Policies (FM-SDFT)

2026

Capstone research · February – July

GitHub · FM-SDFTGitHub · Replay baseline

Studied how robot policies can learn new tasks while retaining earlier ones. Adapted self-distillation fine-tuning to continuous-action policies by matching teacher and student predictions in flow-matching velocity space.

Reported averages across Spatial, Goal and Long · Table 6 (pp. 11–12)
Training methodSuccess rate ↑Forgetting (NBT) ↓
Experience replay72.0%0.223
Experience replay + FM-SDFT80.5%0.171
Pretraining + replay79.0%0.115
Pretraining + replay + FM-SDFT82.3%0.075

The combined method achieves the highest reported average success and lowest average forgetting in Table 6. Each suite contains 10 sequentially learned tasks; the final experiments use CLIP text embeddings and a distillation weight of λ = 0.1.

Training design and evaluation

The implementation combines a frozen previous-task teacher, Euler-integrated action trajectories, and a task-balanced experience replay buffer. Experiments separate the effects of pretraining, replay, and self-distillation across LIBERO task suites.

  • Flow matching: replace the DDPM noise-prediction objective with continuous-time velocity prediction and use 10-step Euler integration for action generation.
  • Self-distillation: generate trajectories with the frozen teacher, interpolate between noise and the generated trajectory, and penalize disagreement between student and teacher velocities.
  • Replay: distribute a 1,000-sample memory budget across previously seen tasks, rebalance it at task boundaries, and mix replay and current-task examples equally.
  • Ablations: compare pretraining only, replay only, pretraining plus replay, and additional self-distillation. Evaluate saved checkpoints with success-rate matrices and forgetting metrics.
  • Reproducibility: the repositories include suite configurations, sequential-training scripts, rollout evaluation, and batch execution support for Slurm and Singularity.

SafeSwitch

2026

Large Language Models course project · March – June

GitHubReported results

Conditional activation steering for Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama-3.2-1B. Two probes estimate whether a prompt is harmful and whether the model is already likely to refuse it. Their combined gate controls the strength of a learned steering vector during generation.

PyTorch · Transformers · Linear probes · Activation steering · Ablation studies

Gating method and evaluation results

Compared unmodified models, always-on steering, and conditional steering across harmful, jailbreak, and benign prompts. The released results show higher jailbreak refusal with unchanged benign compliance under conditional steering.

The gate increases with estimated harmfulness and decreases when the model is already likely to refuse. A forward hook adds the scaled steering vector to the selected transformer layer; the same pipeline supports always-on and no-steering ablations.

Reported evaluation · Baseline → SafeSwitch
ModelJailbreak refusalBenign compliance
Llama-3.2-1B80% → 86%98% → 98%
Qwen2.5-1.5B76% → 86%96% → 96%

Each model is evaluated on 50 prompts per split. The implementation labels steered refusals with a text-pattern heuristic, and benign compliance is defined as one minus refusal rate rather than a measure of answer quality.
